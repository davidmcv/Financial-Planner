Funding & smoothing

When on, cash, ISAs, GIAs, tax-free lump sums and any downsizing proceeds are drawn to top income up to your target — flattening the income line and the later drop-off. The breakdown chart shows exactly where each year's money comes from.

Tip: the red target line on the chart above is draggable — grab it to sweep your income target up and down; pension income and savings are fitted below it to fill any funding gaps. Double-click the chart to reset to the living-standard target.

Your events

One-off amounts in a given year (an inheritance in, a wedding or house purchase out). They adjust that year's income line and appear as flags on the chart.

Market stress test (Monte Carlo)

—

Replays your household plan against ~100 years of (approximate) real annual returns for a UK/global equity portfolio, resampled in random multi-year blocks, to ask: how often does the money last? Withdrawals each year = your spending target minus guaranteed income (State + DB). Historical patterns are no guarantee of the future.

Blank = use living-standard target above

Longevity chart (will the money outlast you?)

—

Plots, across age to the maximum modelled age: your chance of still being alive, the Monte Carlo portfolio success rate (money still lasts to that age), and a longevity-adjusted rate that weights success by the chance you're alive to need it.

What-if scenarios

Compare tweaks against your base plan — retire earlier or later, weaker or stronger markets, spend more or less. The chart above overlays each scenario's household income.

Tax strategies (and their inheritance-tax impact)

Model common decumulation moves and see the honest trade-off: income-tax efficiency now versus Inheritance Tax later. Available for you and, where relevant, your spouse.

Takes your tax-free lump sum (up to £268,275) at your pension access age and invests it in a General Investment Account in your spouse's name, so future gains are taxed at their lower rate.
From retirement, moved into a Stocks & Shares ISA for tax-free growth (25% tax-free, 75% taxed as income)
A non-taxpayer can pay £2,880 a year into a pension and HMRC adds 20% basic-rate relief — £720 — grossing it to £3,600, even with no earnings. That £720/yr is free money. Only while your total taxable income stays under the £12,570 personal allowance: above that, the tax you'd pay drawing it back out cancels the relief, so the app won't count it.
Fully permitted — you can pay into your spouse's pension and HMRC adds 20% relief at source. Most effective when they're a non-/basic-rate taxpayer (the relief is free money). If they're a higher-rate taxpayer they can claim further relief via self-assessment. A non-earner is capped at £2,880 net (£3,600 gross).
