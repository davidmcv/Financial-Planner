Paying in Your pot & how it grows

% you take out each year in retirement
Reduces the drawdown pot to 75% and adds the tax-free cash to your savings, available to fund/smooth retirement. Instead of leaving the cash sitting there, it moves into ISAs at up to £20,000 per person a year, where it grows free of tax on the way out. Taking it early also fixes the amount before further pot growth — so it is a trade, not free money.

🔄 Keep paying into a pension after you retire

You can carry on paying into a pension after you stop working, and the provider still adds 20% basic-rate relief — up to £3,600 gross a year (£2,880 from your pocket) even with no earnings at all, right up to age 75. Turn it on for either of you and the plan below shows whether it is actually worth doing.

The money leaves your savings and lands in your pension grossed up by the 20% relief. Capped at £2,880 net once you have no earnings, and stops at 75.
Paying into someone else's pension is fully permitted — the relief follows their tax position, not yours, so it works best when they are a non- or basic-rate taxpayer.
